How it works

StockArithm takes two broad kinds of signal ideas - traditional ETF rotation and alternative-data inputs - and runs them through the same public test.

Traditional signals use familiar market logic like momentum, relative strength, and rotation. Alternative-data signals use real-world inputs like TSA traffic, freight movement, electricity demand, and search behavior to see whether those inputs improve the odds enough to matter.

Each signal is paper-traded, ranked against SPY, and kept visible whether it wins or loses. The point is not the prettiest backtest. The point is to show which signals are actually surviving in public, under the same scoreboard, with the losers still on the page.

Why it matters

Most trading signals look good until they have to survive in public. StockArithm exists to show that survival test in real time so you can decide what is worth your money.

One example

If TSA volume spikes, the lab can turn that into a travel or consumer signal, run it against SPY, and show whether it actually helps you get ahead of the move.
